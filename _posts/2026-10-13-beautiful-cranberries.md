I love cranberries.  It is now their time.  


## The Other Autumnal Beauty: The Cranberry Harvest  

Everybody yacks about the autumnal beauty of leaves in New England, where to go and when
to see them best.  

Well, most people do.  One person of my acquaintance came from the American west.  They
were used to plains, rocky canyons, and mountains almost devoid of trees.  That _was_ the
scenery for them, and they complained that in New England, they "couldn't see the scenery
because of all these trees in the way".  They did not elect to stay in New England.

And the leaves _are_ quite beautiful, as we've previously noted on this Crummy Little Blog
That Nobody Reads (CLBTNR).  But there's another sort of autumnal beauty, often
overlooked, and rather specialized in nature: the cranberry harvest.  The venerable
_Globe_ this week reminded us with a photo essay by John
Tlumacki. <sup id="fn1a">[[1]](#fn1)</sup>

These are just a few of his photos, by all means go to the source to see more.  I love the
bright red colors, the contrast with the still-green trees, the water, and&hellip; well,
_everything_ about this scene.  If you're there in person, the autumnal forest smells are pretty
great, too.  

What's not depicted in the photos is the amount of physical labor this demands from the
workers, standing in cold water.  Have a moment of sympathy for them, and hope that your
cranberry-buying funds go at least partly to the workers and not the combine and
wholesalers between you &amp; them!  

Tart cranberry bread, cranberry terrine, cranberry fruit relish&hellip;  It's time.  


## The Weekend Conclusion  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-13-beautiful-cranberries-publishers-consider-thanksgiving.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-13-beautiful-cranberries-publishers-consider-thanksgiving.jpg" width="400" height="300" alt="The Weekend Publishers, 2 housecats, are attempting to confer, consult, and otherwise hob-knob with 5 wild turkeys." title="The Weekend Publishers, 2 housecats, are attempting to confer, consult, and otherwise hob-knob with 5 wild turkeys." style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
There are many measures of season.  

We gauge autumn in particular by the changes in forest leaves, the drop in temperature and
coming of frost, the elections
([hey, if you're a US voter, have you done that yet?!](https://www.someweekendreading.blog/voted-2026-midterms/)),
the foods, and many things.  

Here, for example, you see the Weekend Publishers parked on the enclosed back deck, hoping
to murder some local fauna.  They are anticipating Thanksgiving, and would like the
turkeys to believe that the cats just want
[to confer, consult, and otherwise hob-knob](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/MovieSpeeches/moviespeechthewizardofozballoonbye.html)
with them about their holiday plans.  The turkeys understand and appreciate the glass; the
cats do not.  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-13-beautiful-cranberries-publishers-take-over-the-bed.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-13-beautiful-cranberries-publishers-take-over-the-bed-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="533" alt="The Assistant Weekend Publisher (foreground) and the Weekend Publisher (background) take over the bed, preventing a morning sleep-in" title="The Assistant Weekend Publisher (foreground) and the Weekend Publisher (background) take over the bed, preventing a morning sleep-in" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
Another aspect of autumn is that the cats are much more interested in cuddling when the
weather is cooler, and they can't just park for the day on the enclosed back deck,
menacing the local fauna with their supposed alpha predator status.  Instead, they want to
keep close and keep warm.  

This morning, I got up momentarily for standard maintenance, intending to go back to bed
for some more delicious morning sleep, one of the deep benefits of retirement.  Alas, upon
my return, I found these 2 hooligans had taken over the bed, sucking up all the remaining
warmth for themselves.  They were disinclined to make room for me.

This CLBTNR has in its motto, "Occasional tart thoughts&hellip;", and we do really mean
that.  However, in a more literal sense, I now look forward to a autumn of much tart
baking, and my very favorite _very_ tart cranberry fruit terrine for a holiday table.  

May we all look forward to a good autumn, and a good election.  If you're a US eligible voter,
go get that taken care of, ok?
