Here in New England, we got some serious smoke from the Canadian wildfires. How does it
compare to other places?  


## Interplanetary Smoke  

On 2026-Jul-15, smoke from some extensive Canadian wildfires blew down into the US,
creating some surreal atmospheric color.  (Also surreal: Trump, being an idiot, decided it
was because Canadians didn't properly rake leaves in their forests, and vowed to impose
50% punitive tariffs.)  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-22-boston-interplanetary-smoke-boston.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-22-boston-interplanetary-smoke-boston-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="533" alt="Sun in a Boston suburb in late afternoon, looking like Darkover" title="Sun in a Boston suburb in late afternoon, looking like Darkover" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
Californians are used to such things, but in New England that much smoke is very much a
bizarre and different experience.  The sky took on a deep color, and the mid-afternoon
light was an ominous, lowering yellow like a hurricane was about to pass through.  Not
weather, just smoke.  For us, thankfully, it was mostly in the upper atmosphere and not
down here on the ground.  

This photo was taken by the Weekend Editrix in late afternoon.  It shows the sun as a
glaring red star, almost like we're all suddenly in
[a Darkover novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkover_series).  Darkover novels are no
longer _au courant_ in F/SF fandom due to the unfortunate facts revealed about their
author after her death.  But, many years ago, they struck a romantic chord in me.  (Ok,
_some_ of them.  Others were more cathartic than dramatic, possibly constituting
self-therapy for the author.)  

Of course, I was not the only person to note this!  

<a href="https://bsky.app/profile/theplanetaryguy.com/post/3mqpj6ml3422w"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-22-boston-interplanetary-smoke-comparison.jpg" width="550" height="528" alt="Comparison of 'smoky' images from Venus, Titan, and Boston" title="Comparison of 'smoky' images from Venus, Titan, and Boston" style="margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000; margin: 0 auto; display: block;"></a>
Over on BlueSky, Paul Byrne's post of this triptych of yellow-skied pictures definitely
caught my eye.  They are, respectively, photos from the surface of Venus by one of the
Russian probes, the surface of Saturn's moon Titan, and&hellip; Boston.  

The average ambient surface temperatures are, of course, _not_ comparable:  
- Venus: +470&deg;C  
- Titan: &nbsp;&nbsp;–180&deg;C  
- Earth (Boston, specifically): 11&deg;C (though about 35&deg;C on this particular day)  

Alas, as pointed out by 'Notorious RBMK' (great name, RBMK being the class of Soviet
nuclear reactors with a history of safety problems, including the Chernobyl meltdown) on
BlueSky, the Venus image appears to have been 'doctored up' from real images and some
imagination.  

It turns out the Planetary Society has 
[a collection of every picture from Venus, ever](https://www.planetary.org/articles/every-picture-from-venus-surface-ever).
(It also turns out there are only 6 of them, so you can examine each photo in detail, if you
are of a mind to do so.  Note to future photographic forgers: if there are only 6 known
true photos, _maybe your fake will be quickly detected?_)  The photo shown here appears to
have started from the Venera 13 rear camera panorama, and then the rest was faked and
added somehow.  I'm deeply suspicious there was some AI slop going on here, added by one
of the crappy LLMs with which we are all now afflicted.  

Still&hellip; consider the point made: it was a very weird weather day.  

If you need the point made more sharply, consider this video from someone who works as a
train conductor &amp; engineer in Canada.  It shows his train in Ontario, 
_driving through the fire,_ urging whatever's ahead to get the heck out of they way so
they can move:  

<a href="https://bsky.app/profile/geekyjules.julessherred.com/post/3mqnpk453o22z"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-22-boston-interplanetary-smoke-cn-train.jpg" width="550" height="600" alt="View from a train in Ontario, driving THROUGH the wildfires, with trees burning at close range" title="View from a train in Ontario, driving THROUGH the wildfires, with trees burning at close range" style="margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000; margin: 0 auto; display: block;"></a>
Expect more of this, because it will take &ndash; optimistically &ndash; _decades_ to decarbonize our
societies and get greenhouse gas levels down slowly.  If we don't decarbonize, expect this to get
_rapidly_ worse, and lead to mass deaths in unexpected parts of the world.
