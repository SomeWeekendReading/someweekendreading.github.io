An article earlier this year in _Politico_ included data showing Trump was using disaster
relief funding denials and delays for political punishment.  <sup id="fn1a">[[1]](#fn1)</sup> 
This was recently confirmed by some _Associated Press_ reporting picked up by Boston's PBS
station, WBUR.  <sup id="fn2a">[[2]](#fn2)</sup>  Let's see what's going on there.  

First, Trump in general seems to be denying state applications for disaster relief at an
overall historical low:  

> President Donald Trump has rejected disaster aid for Democratic-run states at the
> highest rate in the 47-year history of the Federal Emergency Management Agency.  

But his denials have been politically _extremely_ biased, as shown in the plots over time
of the number of grants approved in red/blue states and the delay time involved:  

> He approved just 23 percent of disaster funding requests from states with a Democratic
> governor and two Democratic senators since returning to office 14 months ago. For states
> with a Republican governor and two Republican senators, it’s the opposite — Trump has
> approved 89 percent of their requests.  

This is historically unprecedented since the founding of FEMA in 1979.  It's also highly
distinct even from his first term, when it was 93% vs 89%, i.e., statistically
indistinguishable.  

The time to process the application also indicates significant foot-dragging on any
application from a Democratic state:  

> Trump has taken 80 days on average to approve or deny requests from Democratic-led
> states — compared to 39 days for Republican-led states.  

He has also openly admitted partisan bias, citing his wins in Republican states as reason
to grant applications.  

> “I just approved \\$60.6 million for the Great State of Tennessee (which I won three
> times!)” Trump wrote on Feb. 6.  

> “It is my Honor to deliver for the Great State of Alaska, which I won BIG in 2016,
> 2020, and 2024,” Trump wrote on Oct. 22, 2025, announcing \\$25 million in disaster aid.  

> “I won ‘The Show Me State’ three times in 2016, 2020, and 2024, and it is my Honor to
> deliver for these incredible Patriots!” Trump wrote in an Oct. 22, 2025, post announcing
> \\$2.5 million in disaster aid for Missouri.  

Although nakedly corrupt, this is apparently _prima facie_ legal:
federal law gives the president complete authority over such aid, without review.  As
always, when Trump gets unreviewable power, he turns it to corrupt uses.  

Of course our generally mathematical illiterate press does not report the number of cases
approved or disapproved by various presidents over time, nor their delay times.  This
makes it impossible to assess statistical significance in our usual way, but the graphs
above certainly _look_ damning.  It would be a _stupendous_ surprise if this were not
statistically significant!
