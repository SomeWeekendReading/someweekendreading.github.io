I voted in the US 2026 midterms!  (Can you guess which way?)  


## Voting by Mail  

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-09-voted-2026-midterms-midterm-vote-1.jpg" width="400" height="155" alt="Secretary of the Commonwealth: Ballot mailed" title="Secretary of the Commonwealth: Ballot mailed" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;">
Like many other voters, I've requested a mail-in ballot.  If I recall correctly, there was
a way to say "from now on" in that request, and I did so.  

Periodically I check my voter registration and mail-in ballot status, because there's been
so much shenanigans perpetrated by Republicans seeking to disenfranchise voters.  That's
not so much the case here in Massachusetts, but I at least want to model correct behavior by
checking weekly in the couple months before the election.  

So at the beginning of his week, I was happy to see the Secretary of the Commonwealth's
web site had my data as shown here: ballot mailed out, not yet returned.  I could expect
to receive it in probably 2-3 days.  

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-09-voted-2026-midterms-midterm-vote-2.jpg" width="400" height="211" alt="Ballot arrived" title="Ballot arrived" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;">
And on Wednesday, here it was (suitably anonymized because&hellip; well, you know why
&mdash; these are the times in which we live.)  Inside there was a letter of instruction,
2 pages of ballot, a ballot envelope, and a return envelope.  

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-09-voted-2026-midterms-midterm-vote-3.jpg" width="400" height="533" alt="Ballot going into drop-box" title="Ballot going into drop-box" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;">
That was all pretty straightforward, so I filled it all out carefully, put the ballot in
the ballot envelope with my signature, and put it in the return envelope with my return
address on it.  

I did not, however, mail it.  There's been too much in the way of Republican threats about
postal delivery of ballots for me to relax doing that.  

So I walked it the mile or so to my local town office, and found their drop-box.  These
are always under video surveillance, because in some states there have been goons
"guarding" the drop-boxes intimidating anyone not sufficiently white who approached.  In my
particular situation, there was video surveillance, the town office only a few meters
away, and a police station across the street.  

So, as you can see here, I felt very confident that I could submit my ballot here and
cause the correct thing to happen.  

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-10-09-voted-2026-midterms-midterm-vote-4.jpg" width="400" height="159" alt="Secretary of the Commonwealth: Ballot accepted" title="Secretary of the Commonwealth: Ballot accepted" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;">

Then began the checking phase, in which I attempted to be _sure_ that the ballot was
received, judged to be in good order, and queued for counting.  We never used to have to
do that, but see "Republican shenanigans" above.  I'm almost certainly being
over-protective here in deep blue Massachusetts, but again I want to model correct
behavior.  

Two days later, the Secretary of the Commonwealth's web site updated as shown here: ballot
received as of October 9, found to be in good order, and ready for counting.  

This means my vote is now locked in.  I can stop worrying about fulfilling my personal
responsibility, and begin worrying about everybody else fulfilling theirs!  


## The Massachusetts Ballot Initiatives  

There were 9 ballot initiatives this time.  I know that's not very many by California
standards, but it's a lot for us.  As I didn't get the DSA guidance in time (again!), I
just looked at the official summary and the pro/con statements by interest groups.  

Here's where I landed:  

- __Question 1:__ _Expanding the public records law_ Basically, should more information be
  available to the public, or not?  Hard to argue against that! Secrecy seems to bring out
  the worst in politicians and agencies.  So that's a YES.  
- __Question 2:__ _Collective bargaining for CPCS Employees_ Should we make it utterly and
  completely clear that some judicial branch employees can form a union?  Unions good.  So
  that's another clear YES.  
- __Question 3:__ _State primary elections_ Somebody wants to replace party primaries with
  something like a "jungle primary", in which everybody runs against everybody else
  regardless of parties, and the top 2 go to a runoff in the main election.  It's not
  _quite_ rank preference voting, which I would love (but which was previously voted
  down).  The argument against is 2-fold:  
  - Minorities tend to vote in the Democratic primary, and their voices would be diluted
    by Republican votes, which sadly tend to be anti-minority.  Not sure how I feel about
    this, but ok.  
  - Most importantly to me, it is backed by billionaire bros.  It has attracted so much of
    the powerful and monied folks that it has their stink about it.  I don't know exactly
    _how_ they plan on screwing everybody else with this, but I'm reasonably certain
    that's a major probability.  

  So, based mostly on the support by very, very rich people, that's a NO.  
- __Question 4:__ _Election day registration_ Should people be able to register at the
    last minute?  If they're willing to sign the usual oath backed by felony penalties,
    sure.  Impediments to voter registration or voting are almost _always_ bad.  So this
    one is a firm YES.  
- __Question 5:__ _State revenue limit &amp; rebate_ Should we put a cap on tax money
    flowing into the government, and force automatic rebates?  C'mon&hellip; this is just
    stupid.  We _already_ have ways to lobby politicians to adjust taxes.  A firm NO.  
- __Question 6:__ _Natural Resource Conservation Fund_ Should we put some money in a fund
    to protect clean air &amp; water, reduce pollution and the like?  The only downside I
    can find is it is not _new_ money, but a diversion of a small amount from funds that
    usually go to other agencies &amp; towns.  Well, if they're _not_ doing their job to
    preserve the health of our living spaces, then put the money where it _will_ happen.
    That's a YES.  
- __Question 7:__ _Single family homes_ Should we, state-wide, require zoning laws to
    allow construction of small homes on small lots?  Very many towns block this, because
    our local zoning laws are very, very zany.  We need more housing, particularly
    "starter" homes.  A firm YES.  
- __Question 8:__ _Prohibit retail sale of adult recreational use of marijuana_ Should we
    undo legalization?  No, let's not go back to training people to seek it illegally, from
    unsafe sources, and learning contempt for law.  I'm not _happy_ about marijuana, but I
    see it about like alcohol: people _will_ have this, one way or another, so we might as
    well make sure it's safe, put in place some laws about intoxication, and collect some
    taxes.  So that's a NO.  
- __Question 9:__ _Firearms regulation_ The legislature enacted a law regulating and
  tracking some kinds of firearms like ghost guns and assault weapons.  Weirdly worded, a
  "yes" vote keeps the legislation, and a "no" vote repeals it.  I hate guns.  I want to
  see them limited to target shooting ranges or hunting, and pretty much nothing else.  So
  this was a YES.  

Perhaps the DSA will get around to mailing me their opinion sometime soon, so I can check
my answers against theirs.  (_Note to DSA folk:_ Work faster!  You're useless if you don't
get your voter guides out in time.)
