Today I voted in the primary.  


## Voting by Mail  

Lately, Trump has been trying to restrict or eliminate voting by mail.  In particular,
he's been trying to get the Post Office to refuse to deliver ballots, but only in states
that did not surrender their voting info to him, so he can purge/disenfranchise
Democrats.  

Fortunately, pro-democracy organizations like Marc Elias and Democracy Docket have been
fighting this in court, and have won 18 out of 18 times so far:  

<a href="https://bsky.app/profile/marcelias.bsky.social/post/3mrsy3d4dns2r"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-elias-1.jpg" width="550" height="600" alt="Marc Elias (Democracy Docket) @ BlueSky: 18/18 losses by Trump DOJ seeking state voter info for purges" title="Marc Elias (Democracy Docket) @ BlueSky: 18/18 losses by Trump DOJ seeking state voter info for purges" style="margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000; margin: 0 auto; display: block;"></a>

So here in Massachusetts, I got my mail-in ballot for the September Democratic primary
today.  Of course I filled it out immediately!  

But&hellip; to avoid Post Office shenanigans, I took it directly to our Town Clerk's
office.  Normally I'd show a photo of my ugly ol' hairy hand stuffing it in the drop-box.
But I got a bit confused about _which_ drop-box, so I went inside to the clerk's office to
ask, where they graciously took it directly.  (I was too embarrassed to ask to photograph them
taking it.)  

## The Only Part That (Probably) Matters  

This is a Democratic primary in Massachusetts, so it's only to be expected that many
Democrats were not opposed within their own party.  The interesting race though was for US
Senator, between incumbent Sen Ed Markey and challenger Rep Seth Moulton.  My take:  
- Markey is old, but is a reliable liberal.
- Moulton is younger, but very much _not_ a reliable liberal vote.  He's more of a squishy
  'centrist', who waffles on trans rights, holds millions in investments in military
  contractors with business before the government, does not support Medicare for All, and
  so on.  
  
In the end, I went for Markey.  I'm sick and tired of waffling Democratic 'centrists' who
sabotage all the progressive polices I want, like Manchin & Sinema did during the Biden
administration.  Yes, I despise the gerontocracy of our elderly leadership.  But some degree
of commitment to sane and humane policy is far more important to me.  

So Markey got my primary vote.  

The _important_ vote will be in November, to make sure no Republicans within my blast
radius get _any_ votes at all.  Not for any imaginable office, not under any conceivable
circumstance.

## Addendum 2026-Aug-09: Comparing with the DSA Recommendations  

When I first heard of the [Democratic Socialists of America (DSA)](https://www.dsausa.org/)
some years ago, my first thought was that their name was pure rage-bait for Republicans.
But then, pretty much _everything_ pro-democracy causes Republican rage these days.  I looked down their
list of policy preferences and agreed with maybe 80% or 90% of what they advocated, and so
filed them away as a useful source of recommendations in the future.   

Today somebody pointed me at the [Democratic primary voting recommendations](https://docs.google.com/document/d/16hEYmUFJFfVU-7rqYJEMpm99EdZKgB6UjvUF8piXovU/edit?pli=1&tab=t.0#heading=h.2qcxxhqq9z93)
for Massachusetts voters from the DSA. (Obviously, there is no set of DSA _Republican_
recommendations, beyond "please don't do that".)  

Let's look at their choices versus the ones I remember making:  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-1.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-1-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="338" alt="Mass DSA: Markey over Moulton for Senate" title="Mass DSA: Markey over Moulton for Senate" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
We agree on Markey over Moulton, for approximately the same reasons.  Markey, now age 80,
is an example of the irritatingly aged Democratic gerontocracy that just _cannot_ let go
of power.  Moulton is younger.  But therewith ends the list of Moulton's virtues: he loses
on LGBTQ rights, has fecklessly recommended Democrats throw trans people under the bus in
the name of illusory centrism, seems a fan of the Israeli genocide in Gaza, and so on.  

So&hellip; in DSA's words, choosing Markey: "This is not a difficult choice."  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-2.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-2-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="188" alt="Mass DSA: Either challenger over Clark?" title="Mass DSA: Either challenger over Clark?" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
For Representative, here in Massachusetts 5, I voted for Clark, the incumbent.  I knew she
was entrenched in the Democratic leadership whose lack of fight I despise, but to pick a
new representative would mean losing a voice with seniority.  Also, I hadn't even _heard_
of her two challengers.  

Looking at the scorecard, it seems either of the two challengers would have been better.
But&hellip; giving up a senior seat in favor of an unknown who has somehow avoided even
appearing on my radar?  That seems risky.  Honestly, after reading the DSA recommendations
here, I could have gone either way.  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-3.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-3-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="161" alt="Mass DSA: Lander over Brownsberger.  Oops." title="Mass DSA: Lander over Brownsberger.  Oops." style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
In the Mass State Senate race, I chose Brownsberger over Lander.  That's based on the fact
that I didn't know much about Lander, but had &mdash; very briefly and indirectly &mdash;
interacted with Brownsberger a couple times.  Brownsberger _does_ have reasonable
constituent outreach.  

But, some years ago, when he ran for the US House, I had an annoying experience with him.
He said in some press release or other that he'd work very hard to be bipartisan and
cooperate with Republicans.  This was _anathema_ to me; you _cannot_ work with people who
will not accept your legitimacy and who advocate policies that seem to me to be outright
evil.  It was just _pre-emptive_ caving in.  I said as much to a friend who was one of his
campaign staffers.  That percolated up the chain and resulted in an email to me from
Brownsberger saying in essence: "What's wrong with planning to work with Republicans?"
Buddy&hellip; if you gotta ask, you ain't gonna understand the answer.  

So, in retrospect, if I'd seen this DSA sheet beforehand, it would have tipped me toward
Lander.  _Sigh._  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-4.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-07-30-voted-2026-primary-dsa-4-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="151" alt="Mass DSA: Rogers over Ganguly" title="Mass DSA: Rogers over Ganguly" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
As for the Mass House race in my district, I honestly don't remember?  I _think_ I voted
for Rogers over Ganguly, but didn't have a lot of information to go on.  This DSA
recommendation sheet would have been useful in solidifying my vote for Rogers.  Seems like
Ganguly didn't even bother with taking positions for the DSA to evaluate!  

I _really_ wish I'd seen this earlier, since I probably would have reconsidered one or two
of my primary votes based on their research.  But&hellip; speed seemed important too.  

Advice to Mass DSA folk: Get your recommendations out earlier and louder in the future!  You
might pick up a few more early voters.  

Well enough, though.
