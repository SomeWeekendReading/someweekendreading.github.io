Every couple years, the battery on my now-antiquated electric lawn mower stops taking a
charge.  It's been a couple years since last time, so&hellip;  


## The Third Session with the Lawn Mower Battery Surgeon   

### The Venerable Black &amp; Decker SPCM1936

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2022-06-24-mower-battery-surgery-mower.jpg" width="400" height="400" alt="Black &amp; Decker SPCM1936 19in 36v self-propelled lawn mower" title="Black &amp; Decker SPCM1936 19in 36v self-propelled lawn mower" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;">
[It is a truth universally acknowledged](https://www.janeausten.org/pride-and-prejudice/chapter-1.php#:~:text=It%20is%20a%20truth%20universally%20acknowledged%2C%20that%20a%20single%20man%20in%20possession%20of%20a%20good%20fortune%2C%20must%20be%20in%20want%20of%20a%20wife%2E)
that anyone, having acquired a suburban American house, must be in need of a lawn mower.

And so it was, Chez Weekend, with the purchase of Ch&acirc;teau Weekend some 15 years
agone.  The mighty mowing machine in question has been the
[Black &amp; Decker SPCM1936](https://www.amazon.com/SPCM1936-Cordless-Electric-Self-Propelled-Removable/dp/B004JMZH3A)
battery-powered self-propelled lawn mower.  We have no particular complaints about the
machine, beyond 2 facts:  
1. It is no longer made, so there are no replacement parts to be had anywhere.  
2. The _battery_ is also no longer made, so when it gives out, things become difficult.  

It's worth pointing out: batteries are _consumables_, eventually ceasing to function
because of the formation of internal dendrites or the like.  If you don't manufacture
replacement batteries, the tools dependent on them become _useless._  If you're a
manufacturer, no doubt you're hoping consumers will just buy newer equipment from you,
ensuring repeat sales of big-ticket items.  

If you're a consumer, you may have other ideas on this subject, some of them rather testy.  

We've had a snowblower become useless that way, so we replaced it with another model from
a company whose batteries fit _all_ their tools.  So right now, the snowblower, leaf
blower, and string trimmer all use the same battery form factor.  They can't stop
manufacturing the battery unless they trash their _entire product line,_ which is the kind
of leverage I like as a consumer.  If we replace the lawn mower next year, it will be with
a model using _that_ battery system.  

In the meantime, our venerable SPCM1936 is determined to make itself useless.  We managed
to scrounge one replacement battery around 2018, but that one had been sitting on a
warehouse shelf somewhere for years, and hence had dubious battery chemistry.  Since then,
we have twice opened up the battery, yanked the 3 sealed lead-acid cells for recycling,
and replaced them, following in the footsteps of numerous sages of
YouTube. <sup id="fn1a">[[1]](#fn1)</sup> <sup id="fn2a">[[2]](#fn2)</sup>  

This&hellip; works, but is a pain.  

Today we have to do it again, making this our 5th battery in 15 years.  

Say it with me: _batteries are consumables._  

### The Surgery  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-1.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-1-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="309" alt="Battery surgery: mise en place" title="Battery surgery: mise en place" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
After first propitiating the various trickster deities (most of whom are related to
[Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_effect), waiting to pounce upon aging
theoretical physicists attempting anything practical), we began the surgery.  

Here you see our [_mise en place_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mise_en_placee).  Unlike
the previous time, we were not this time under the supervision of the Assistant Weekend
Publisher &mdash; the junior cat Chez Weekend &mdash; as he was assisting the Weekend
Publisher in some urgent cat-napping business.  So nobody was in Cat Jail this time, eager
to bat small parts into dark basement corners under heavy cabinets.  

- Top center is the main attraction, our patient [etherized upon a table](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poetrymagazine/poems/44212/the-love-song-of-j-alfred-prufrock#:~:text=Let%20us%20go,upon%20a%20table%3B),
  ready for organ replacements.  
- To the right are 3 replacement organs, in this case 12 volt sealed lead-acid batteries
  to be wired in series for 36 volt service.  
- The surgical tools include torx drivers (T20 is the one you want), some pliers and WD-40
  in case of recalcitrant parts, and a utility knife with duct tape. Because&hellip; _duct tape._  
- Also, because I am not foolish, or at least not foolish in _that_ way, there is a
  printout of our escapades in battery surgery from 2 years ago, to remind us where the
  various internal organs are located and what hazards lie in wait.  
- A small caffeine refueling tank, in the form of Diet Coke, is for your humble Weekend
  Editor, who also runs out of charge pretty easily nowadays.  
- As always, the venerable _Globe_ undergirds all.  

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-2.jpg" width="400" height="205" alt="Battery surgery: screws extracted, laid out geometrically by whence they came" title="Battery surgery: screws extracted, laid out geometrically by whence they came" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;">
<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-3.jpg" width="400" height="397" alt="Battery surgery: bottom view, including strange drain hole?" title="Battery surgery: bottom view, including strange drain hole?" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;">
The next step, of course, is the initial incision &mdash; or in this case, turning the
battery upside-down and removing the screws holding the lid shut with the T20 torx driver.  We
lay them out as shown, in the same geometry as they go back on the lid at the end.  Note
that they are of different lengths, so we _would_ confuse this if offered the
opportunity.  We always respect
[the wisdom of Sgt Oddball, played by Donald Sutherland in the 1970 movie _Kelly's Heroes_, about getting
out of trouble _quicker_ than we got into it.](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0065938/characters/nm0000661#:~:text=Oddball%3A%20These%20engines%20are%20the%20fastest%20in%20any%20tanks%20in%20the%20European%20Theater%20of%20Operations%2C%20forwards%20or%20backwards%2E%20You%20see%2C%20man%2C%20we%20like%20to%20feel%20we%20can%20get%20out%20of%20trouble%2C%20quicker%20than%20we%20got%20into%20it)  

(Ask me sometime how long it took me to learn _that_ lesson.)  

Backing up a bit to look at the larger picture of the bottom of the battery, we noticed
something peculiar.  In the lower right of the picture, there's a _drainage hole_ in the
bottom of the battery casing.  Now, the lid is screwed on tight with a rubber gasket, so
it can't be they think it's gonna fill with rain water.  Somebody decided, if the
batteries leak, the liquid needs to drain out of the battery.  So far, so good.  But the
genius who decided that also decided to leak battery acid _on the lawn mower underneath,_
which is much more expensive than the battery!  

I really do wonder about engineers, sometimes.  Most times, really.  

(Like the guy who invented the torx: do we really need something in addition to flathead
and Phillips head screwdrivers, and _incompatibly different?_  No, we do not.  But, as all
the existentialists say: nevertheless, here we are.)  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-4.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-4-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="447" alt="Battery surgery: a view of the innards" title="Battery surgery: a view of the innards" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-5-6.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-5-6-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="600" alt="Battery surgery: Front &amp; back views of which color wires attach to which color terminals" title="Battery surgery: Front &amp; back views of which color wires attach to which color terminals" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
Next, we _carefully_ turn the battery right-side up, taking care not to spill the guts.  

Here's a view from the top, showing the 3 cells and the little circuit board built into
the lid.  Oddly, it appears the old cells are not swollen, like their predecessors.  But
at least nobody's leaking.  

Because I'm a nasty suspicious old bastard, here _also_ are pictures taken from the front
and the back, showing which colors of wires connect to which colors of terminals.
Honestly, a lot of this is just about not offering yourself opportunities to do Dumb
Stuff.  As we noted last time:  

> - __Front cell:__ red terminal on the right, two black wires connecting routed from each
>   side.  
> - __Back 2 cells:__ red terminals on the left (looking down, remember this on the other side
>   so the cells have been rotated). Black wire comes around on the left and acquires a
>   red jacket, other black wire comes around on the right and stays black. Green and
>   white wires connect to the middle terminals, as shown.  
> - Somewhat harder to see: in the gray clip just above the cells, there’s a small metal
>   rod that holds the clip in position. If you look at it sideways, it will fall out and
>   roll obstinately under some inconveniently heavy object. Do not permit it to do that.  

After that, the surgery was straightforward, though one should always be careful not to
commit the crime of Bad Touch on the battery terminals.  Yes, it's _supposed_ to be an
open circuit.  But I have only 1 heart to use as a circuit tester, and I'm not going to
risk using it up on _this._  

Upon reassembly, pushing the charge indicator on the top showed 3 lights.  As terrible an
approximation to battery state as that is, it's what the manufacturer made and so what we
believe.  


## The Weekend Conclusion  

<a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-7.jpg"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/2026-08-23-mower-battery-surgery-3-7-thumb.jpg" width="400" height="300" alt="The Assistant Weekend Publisher reminds us that there are better priorities to which we need attend, like loving cats" title="The Assistant Weekend Publisher reminds us that there are better priorities to which we need attend, like loving cats" style="float: right; margin: 3px 3px 3px 3px; border: 1px solid #000000;"></a>
The patient lived, and showed good appetite post-surgery by mowing tall, wet grass. Best I
can ask of a 15 year old mower with its brave new little battery transplant.  

Though the Assistant Weekend Publisher was not available to be Attending Cat in the
surgery, he was available for valuable editorial assistance in writing this blog post.  He
wishes to remind everyone that there are often higher priorities, such as properly loving
one's cat.  

He is correct.
